For traffic arbitrage, finding a new feed isn't simply about finding a partner that can deliver volume.

We need to understand whether the **feed can provide scalable, stable, and monetizable traffic**—and whether that performance remains consistent as volume increases.

Before committing significant traffic to a new partner, we evaluate several factors.

## 1. How Scalable Is the Feed?

The first question is simple:

**How much traffic can the feed actually handle?**

A feed may perform extremely well at a relatively small volume but become unstable as traffic increases.

We therefore evaluate:

- Available daily volume
- How quickly traffic can be scaled
- Performance at different traffic levels
- Traffic consistency
- Available Tier 1 geos
- Whether traffic quality changes as volume increases
- Whether RPM/RPC remains sustainable while scaling

A feed isn't truly scalable simply because it can technically send more traffic.

**Real scalability means increasing volume without destroying the economics of the funnel.**

-----

## 2. How Stable Are RPMs Throughout the Day?

Daily average RPM can hide significant fluctuations.

Two feeds can produce the same average RPM while behaving completely differently throughout the day.

One might maintain relatively consistent monetization from morning to evening. Another might produce excellent RPM for several hours and then drop substantially.

That's why we monitor performance throughout the day rather than looking only at the final daily average.

We analyze:

**Traffic Volume → RPM/RPC → Revenue → Time of Day**

Understanding these patterns helps us determine when traffic is most valuable and whether a feed can support consistent scaling.

-----

## 3. Domain Design Can Affect Conversion Rate

The feed isn't the only variable.

**The design and structure of the destination domain can also influence user behavior and conversion rate.**

We evaluate factors such as:

- Page structure
- Article layout
- Navigation
- Visual hierarchy
- Mobile experience
- Content placement
- Loading experience

Even relatively small differences in the destination experience can change how users interact with the page.

This is why we evaluate the **feed and the destination environment together**, rather than treating them as completely separate variables.

-----

## 4. Can We Manually Insert Articles?

Another important question is whether we have control over the articles being served.

If we're allowed to manually insert articles, we can build content around topics with stronger commercial intent and adapt the content strategy to current demand.

If manual article insertion isn't available, we need to evaluate the feed's existing article selection much more carefully.

The question becomes:

**Do the articles meet the criteria required to generate the highest possible RPC traffic?**

The quality of the article selection matters because the subject, intent, and relevance of the content can directly influence the type of users entering the funnel and the value generated from that traffic.

For us, editorial control is therefore an important part of evaluating a new feed.

## 5. The Testing Phase

We don't expect a new feed to reveal its full potential from one test.

We use a structured testing phase across different:

**Tier 1 Geos → Service Verticals → Software**

The goal isn't simply to find the highest initial RPM.

We're trying to understand **where the feed performs best and under which conditions.**

A feed can perform differently across:

- Different Tier 1 markets
- Different service verticals
- Different software
- Different content categories
- Different periods of the day

This gives us a performance map rather than a single data point.

## 6. We Follow Market Trends

Feed performance doesn't exist in a vacuum.

Demand for different verticals changes throughout the year—and sometimes changes significantly within a single week.

That's why we follow current market trends and maintain a **demand calendar for our verticals**.

Instead of only asking:

**“Which vertical is performing today?”**

we also ask:

**“What is changing in demand, and what should we be preparing for next?”**

This allows us to move testing resources toward areas where demand is increasing rather than relying entirely on historical performance.

## 7. Day-of-Week Patterns Matter

One of our media buyers took this analysis even further.

After carefully monitoring RPC performance over time, he was able to identify recurring **day-of-week patterns for individual verticals**.

Certain verticals showed noticeable RPC increases on particular days of the week.

That information became useful when planning testing and traffic allocation.

Instead of treating every day as identical, we could use historical observations to understand when certain verticals tend to produce stronger monetization.

This is one of the reasons continuous monitoring is so valuable.

**A single day's performance tells you what happened. Consistent monitoring can reveal when patterns repeat.**

## Our Feed Evaluation Framework

When evaluating a new feed, we look at the entire system:

**Feed Scalability**

↓

**Traffic Quality**

↓

**Domain & Design Performance**

↓

**Article Quality & Control**

↓

**RPM / RPC Stability**

↓

**Geo Performance**

↓

**Vertical Performance**

↓

**Software Performance**

↓

**Time-of-Day & Day-of-Week Patterns**

↓

**Scaling Potential**

The objective isn't simply to find a feed that produces good numbers during an initial test.

We're looking for a **traffic partner whose feed, content, destination environment, and monetization work together at scale.**

Because in arbitrage, the value of a feed isn't determined only by how much traffic it can deliver.

It's determined by whether it can deliver **enough high-quality traffic, with stable monetization, in the right geos and verticals, and at a volume that can actually be scaled.**
