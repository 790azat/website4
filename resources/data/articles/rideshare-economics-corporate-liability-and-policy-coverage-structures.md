Rideshare services have changed how people use personal vehicles for transportation. Drivers can use their own cars to provide paid trips, while transportation network companies (TNCs) connect those drivers with passengers through digital platforms.

This model creates a complicated insurance structure because a driver's vehicle can shift between personal use and commercial activity during the same trip. The applicable coverage may depend on whether the driver is offline, logged into the rideshare platform, waiting for a request, traveling to pick up a passenger, or transporting a passenger.

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) notes that coverage gaps can arise at different stages of the rideshare process because personal auto policies commonly exclude certain commercial or for-hire activities.

Understanding how these coverage structures work can help drivers, passengers, and other people involved in a rideshare accident determine which insurance policies may be relevant.

## 1. What Is a Transportation Network Company?

A transportation network company uses a digital platform to connect passengers with drivers who use personal vehicles to provide transportation for compensation.

Companies operating under this model typically rely on a combination of:

- Driver-provided personal auto insurance
- Rideshare-specific endorsements
- TNC commercial liability policies
- Contingent coverage
- State-mandated insurance requirements

The exact structure varies by company, policy, and state.

Insurance regulators have developed rules governing TNC insurance because ordinary personal auto policies may exclude commercial transportation activity. Nearly all U.S. states and the District of Columbia have enacted some form of legislation addressing TNC insurance requirements.

## 2. Why Rideshare Insurance Is Different

A conventional personal auto policy is primarily designed for personal driving.

Rideshare driving introduces another use: transporting passengers for compensation.

Many personal auto policies contain exclusions related to livery or commercial use. Consequently, a driver's personal policy may not provide the same protection while the vehicle is being used for a rideshare service.

That creates the need to coordinate coverage between the driver's insurer and the TNC.

The central issue is not simply whether the driver has insurance. It is **which policy applies at the precise time of the accident**.

## 3. The Three Common Rideshare Periods

Rideshare insurance is often divided into three operating periods.

### Period 1: App On, Waiting for a Request

The driver is logged into the platform but has not accepted a passenger request.

This period has historically created some of the largest potential coverage gaps.

### Period 2: Request Accepted, Passenger Not Yet Picked Up

The driver has accepted a trip and is traveling toward the passenger.

### Period 3: Passenger in the Vehicle

The driver is transporting the passenger until the trip ends.

The NAIC identifies these three periods as a central feature of rideshare insurance analysis.

Some state laws combine Periods 2 and 3, so the exact terminology and requirements can differ.

## 4. How Corporate Liability Coverage Can Work

TNCs generally maintain commercial liability insurance designed to protect against claims arising from rideshare activity.

According to the NAIC, TNCs commonly provide substantial commercial liability coverage during periods when a driver has accepted a trip or is transporting a passenger. The NAIC describes $1 million in primary commercial liability coverage as a common framework for these periods, although the actual requirements and policy structures vary by jurisdiction and company.

This coverage is primarily concerned with liability to third parties.

For example, if a rideshare driver causes a collision while transporting a passenger, the relevant TNC liability policy may respond to claims for injuries or property damage suffered by other people.

That does not necessarily mean the driver's own vehicle or personal injuries are fully covered by the same policy.

## 5. What Happens During the App-On Waiting Period?

Period 1 can be more complicated.

A driver may be using the vehicle for rideshare purposes but have not yet accepted a trip. State laws can require TNC-related liability coverage during this period, but the amount and structure vary.

The NAIC notes that some state frameworks use minimum liability limits during Period 1 and that rideshare-specific endorsements can be used to address coverage gaps.

For example, California has historically required TNC-related primary liability coverage during the pre-match period, while imposing higher liability requirements once a match has been accepted. California's insurance framework illustrates why state-specific rules matter.

Drivers should therefore avoid assuming that a rideshare company's coverage works identically in every state.

## 6. Liability Coverage vs. Coverage for the Driver's Own Vehicle

One of the most important distinctions in rideshare insurance is between **liability coverage** and **first-party coverage**.

Liability insurance generally addresses damage or injuries that an insured driver causes to other people.

It does not automatically cover:

- Damage to the rideshare driver's own vehicle
- The driver's own medical expenses
- Every uninsured-motorist claim
- Every collision loss

The NAIC notes that TNC policies do not necessarily have to provide medical payments, collision, comprehensive, or uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage.

A driver may therefore need separate coverage or a rideshare endorsement to address these risks.

## 7. Rideshare Endorsements Can Fill Coverage Gaps

Insurance companies have developed rideshare-specific endorsements designed to supplement personal auto coverage.

Depending on the policy, an endorsement may extend certain protection into periods when the driver is logged into the rideshare platform.

Possible benefits can include:

- Liability protection
- Collision coverage
- Comprehensive coverage
- Uninsured or underinsured motorist protection
- Deductible assistance

The specific protections vary significantly by insurer and state.

The NAIC recommends that rideshare drivers discuss their coverage with their insurance provider and investigate products designed to address gaps between personal and TNC policies.

## 8. Who Pays After a Rideshare Accident?

There is no single answer.

The applicable insurance may depend on:

1. Whether the driver was using the rideshare app
2. Whether a trip request had been accepted
3. Whether a passenger was in the vehicle
4. Whether the driver was at fault
5. Whether the driver had a rideshare endorsement
6. The driver's personal policy
7. The TNC's commercial policy
8. State-specific insurance requirements
9. The type and amount of damages

This makes the timing of the accident particularly important.

An accident involving the same vehicle could potentially involve different coverage depending on whether it happened five minutes before a ride request was accepted or while the passenger was already in the vehicle.

## 9. What About an Uninsured Driver?

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage can become relevant when a rideshare vehicle is involved in a collision caused by another driver.

However, the availability of UM/UIM protection during rideshare activity depends on the applicable policies and state law.

The NAIC notes that rideshare-specific policies and endorsements can address coverage gaps involving uninsured or underinsured motorists, but TNC liability policies are not universally required to provide every type of first-party coverage.

A driver should therefore review both the personal policy and the rideshare coverage rather than assuming the TNC's commercial liability policy will handle every loss.

## 10. Why Deductibles Can Matter

A rideshare driver may have a deductible when seeking payment for damage to their own vehicle.

For example, a TNC's contingent collision coverage may apply only if the driver already carries qualifying collision coverage on the personal policy.

The amount of the deductible can also vary between policies.

This means drivers should understand:

- Personal collision deductible
- Rideshare policy deductible
- Whether the TNC provides deductible assistance
- When contingent coverage becomes active
- Whether comprehensive damage is treated differently from collision damage

The NAIC specifically advises drivers to determine whether they could be responsible for a deductible under the TNC's coverage.

## 11. What About Passengers?

Passengers generally have different insurance considerations from drivers.

If a rideshare passenger is injured in a collision, liability coverage associated with the rideshare operation may become relevant, depending on who caused the accident and the applicable policy.

Other potentially relevant coverage can include:

- The driver's liability insurance
- The TNC's commercial liability policy
- Another driver's liability insurance
- Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage
- Medical payments coverage
- Personal injury protection in applicable states

The exact sequence depends on the circumstances and state law.

The NAIC advises rideshare passengers to understand what insurance protection the transportation company provides before using the service.

## 12. Corporate Liability Does Not Mean Unlimited Protection

The presence of a corporate insurance policy does not necessarily mean every accident involving a rideshare vehicle is covered without limits or conditions.

Insurance policies contain:

- Coverage limits
- Deductibles
- Exclusions
- Conditions
- Reporting requirements
- Definitions of covered activities

Additionally, a TNC's commercial liability policy primarily addresses liability risks and should not automatically be treated as comprehensive protection for every person and every type of loss.

California's insurance regulator, for example, has specifically noted that TNC liability policies do not necessarily have to provide collision, comprehensive, medical payments, or UM/UIM protection for drivers.

## 13. Why State Law Matters

Rideshare insurance is regulated at the state level, meaning coverage requirements can differ substantially across jurisdictions.

State law can address:

- Minimum liability limits
- Required TNC coverage periods
- Driver disclosure requirements
- Personal-policy exclusions
- UM/UIM requirements
- Proof-of-insurance requirements
- Claims procedures

The NAIC emphasizes that rideshare insurance availability, coverage, premiums, and requirements vary by state.

For that reason, a rideshare insurance rule from California, New York, or another state should not automatically be treated as the rule everywhere in the United States.

## 14. What Drivers Should Check Before Driving

Before using a personal vehicle for rideshare work, drivers should review both their personal policy and the TNC's insurance information.

Important questions include:

- Does my personal policy permit rideshare use?
- Is a rideshare endorsement available?
- When does the TNC's liability coverage begin?
- What happens while I am waiting for a request?
- What happens after accepting a request?
- Is my own vehicle covered?
- What deductible applies?
- Is comprehensive coverage available?
- Is collision coverage available?
- What UM/UIM protection applies?
- Are there state-specific requirements I need to satisfy?

The NAIC recommends speaking with the personal insurer before driving for a rideshare company and identifying any potential coverage gaps.

## 15. What Accident Victims Should Document

If you are involved in a collision with a rideshare vehicle, document the circumstances as carefully as possible.

Useful information can include:

- Driver's name
- Vehicle information
- License plate
- Rideshare company
- Whether a passenger was present
- Whether the driver was on an active trip
- Photographs of the vehicles and scene
- Witness information
- Police report details
- Medical records and expenses
- Insurance information

If the driver was working for a TNC at the time, that fact can be relevant when determining which insurance policy may apply.

## Final Takeaway

Rideshare insurance is built around the unusual way a personal vehicle transitions between private and commercial use. The applicable coverage can change depending on whether the driver is offline, waiting for a request, traveling to pick up a passenger, or transporting one.

TNC commercial liability policies can provide significant protection, particularly after a trip has been accepted, but they do not necessarily replace the driver's personal auto insurance or provide every type of first-party coverage.

For drivers, reviewing the personal policy, TNC coverage, applicable deductibles, and state requirements before driving can help identify potential gaps. For passengers and other accident victims, documenting the rideshare driver's status and identifying the potentially applicable policies can be important when pursuing a claim.

Because rideshare insurance requirements vary by jurisdiction, the specific policy language and state law should be reviewed before reaching conclusions about coverage.
