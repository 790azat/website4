TikTok traffic is often associated with inexpensive clicks, but there is another characteristic that can make it particularly valuable for traffic arbitrage: **the quality of the users behind those clicks.**

In some of our campaigns, we've observed an interesting pattern:

**Lower CTR + Higher Conversion Rate = More Valuable Traffic**

At first, this can look counterintuitive.

If fewer people are clicking the ad, why would the traffic be more valuable?

The answer is that **click volume and click quality are two different things.**

## TikTok Users Can Be Selective About What They Click

TikTok is fundamentally a content-consumption platform.

Users are scrolling through an enormous amount of content, and most of what they see is never clicked.

When a user decides to leave that experience and interact with an advertisement, there is often a specific reason behind that action.

The creative has successfully created enough interest or curiosity for the user to take the next step.

This can create an interesting funnel:

**Large number of impressions**

↓

**Relatively small percentage clicks**

↓

**Higher proportion of those clicks convert**

That is very different from a campaign where almost everyone clicks but very few users take the next action.

For arbitrage, the second half of the funnel can matter more than the CTR itself.

## Low CTR Doesn't Always Mean Bad Traffic

Media buyers often instinctively look for the highest CTR.

But CTR should always be evaluated together with what happens **after the click**.

Imagine two campaigns:

### Campaign A

100,000 impressions

2,000 clicks

2% CTR

40 conversions

**2% click-to-conversion rate**

### Campaign B

100,000 impressions

1,000 clicks

1% CTR

60 conversions

**6% click-to-conversion rate**

Campaign B generates **half as many clicks**, but it produces **50% more conversions**.

The lower CTR doesn't mean the creative is worse.

It may mean the creative is filtering the audience more effectively.

This is particularly important in traffic arbitrage because the ultimate value of the traffic depends on what happens after the user arrives - not simply how many people click the ad.

## The "Interest Filter"

This is one way we think about TikTok traffic at Impact Waves.

The creative acts as an **interest filter**.

A strong creative doesn't necessarily need to convince everyone to click.

It needs to attract the people who are genuinely interested enough to continue.

The result can look like this:

**Impression → Interest → Click → Engagement → Conversion**

Instead of:

**Impression → Curiosity Click → Immediate Drop-Off**

That distinction can have a major impact on downstream monetization.

When the percentage of users who convert after clicking is high, each individual visitor can become more valuable.

And that is where RPM enters the equation.

## Why This Can Translate Into Higher RPM

RPM ultimately reflects how much revenue is generated per thousand impressions or users, depending on the metric being used.

If TikTok sends traffic that has stronger downstream engagement and conversion behavior, the resulting monetization can be more valuable even when the initial click-through rate isn't exceptionally high.

This is why we don't judge traffic quality using CTR alone.

We look at the complete chain:

**CPM → CTR → CPC → Conversion Rate → Revenue Per Visitor → RPM → ROI**

A cheap click is only useful if the user does something valuable after arriving.

For traffic arbitrage, **the quality of the click can matter more than the quantity of clicks.**

## The Key Metric Isn't "How Many Clicked?"

The more important question is:

**"What did the users who clicked actually do?"**

If a campaign has:

- Lower CTR
- Lower CPC
- Higher conversion rate
- Higher revenue per visitor
- Higher RPM

then reducing the campaign to CTR alone would completely miss the picture.

This is why experienced media buyers often evaluate TikTok traffic differently from traditional performance campaigns.

The objective isn't to maximize every metric individually.

It's to maximize the **economic value of the entire traffic flow.**

## The Arbitrage Advantage

This is where TikTok becomes particularly interesting for arbitrage.

The platform can provide very inexpensive reach, while the users who respond to a sufficiently relevant creative can demonstrate strong downstream intent.

When those two factors meet, the equation becomes extremely attractive:

**Cheap Traffic + High-Intent Clicks + Strong Monetization = Higher Arbitrage Potential**

And that is ultimately what makes TikTok traffic valuable.

**The goal isn't to get the most clicks.**

**The goal is to get the most valuable clicks at the lowest sustainable cost.**
