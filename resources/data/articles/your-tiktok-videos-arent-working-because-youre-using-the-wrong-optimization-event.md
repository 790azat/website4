One of the most overlooked decisions in TikTok media buying is the **optimization event**.

TikTok gives advertisers multiple optimization signals, including actions such as purchases, leads, clicks, and other website interactions.

For many media buyers, choosing the optimization event becomes almost automatic:

**If you want conversions, optimize for Purchase.**

For traffic arbitrage, that logic can be completely wrong.

The optimization event should be determined by **what you actually want the user to do after clicking your ad.**

## What Do You Actually Want From the User?

Before choosing an optimization event, ask a much simpler question:

**What is the next action I want the user to take?**

If your objective is to sell a product, Purchase optimization can make sense.

If your objective is generating a lead, Lead optimization may be more appropriate.

But what if your objective is to send users to an informational article?

What if the value of the traffic comes from users reading the content, exploring related searches, and continuing to interact with the website?

Then optimizing exclusively toward Purchase may be sending TikTok the wrong signal.

The algorithm is going to look for users who resemble the behavior associated with your selected optimization event.

So the first question shouldn't be:

**"Which optimization event is the most valuable?"**

It should be:

**"Which optimization event best represents the behavior I need?"**

## Purchase Optimization Isn't Always the Best Choice for Arbitrage

Purchase optimization can be extremely powerful when the funnel actually ends with a purchase.

But an RSOC-style traffic funnel can work differently.

The advertiser isn't necessarily trying to convince the user to purchase something directly.

The user may first need to:

**See the ad → Click → Read an article → Explore related searches → Engage with the page → Generate monetizable activity**

That's a fundamentally different user journey from:

**See the ad → Click → Buy**

If the first step after the click is **content consumption**, optimizing exclusively around a downstream purchase-type signal may not necessarily produce the audience that performs best throughout the entire content funnel.

This is where many media buyers make a fundamental mistake:

They optimize for the **end of a hypothetical funnel** instead of the **actual behavior that creates value.**

## Optimization Should Follow the Funnel

Consider two different campaigns.

### E-commerce funnel

**Ad → Product Page → Add to Cart → Purchase**

Here, Purchase optimization can be logically aligned with the final objective.

Now consider an informational arbitrage funnel:

**Ad → Article → Content Engagement → Related Search → Monetization**

The desired user behavior is different.

The user isn't necessarily supposed to purchase anything.

They're supposed to be **interested enough to consume the content and continue exploring.**

That changes how you should think about optimization.

The optimization event becomes part of the media-buying strategy rather than simply a technical setting inside the ad platform.

## The Wrong Optimization Event Can Change Your Traffic

This is the part many advertisers underestimate.

Changing the optimization event can change the type of users the platform looks for.

Imagine you have a video that attracts people interested in learning about a particular service.

If your campaign is optimized toward a purchase-related event, TikTok has a reason to search for users whose behavior resembles people likely to complete that event.

But your website may not be selling that service.

Instead, you're sending users to an article designed to inform them and expose them to related searches.

The user who is most likely to **buy** isn't necessarily the same user who is most likely to:

- Read an article
- Scroll through the content
- Explore related searches
- Click another relevant result
- Spend more time on the website

Those are different behaviors.

And in arbitrage, **those behaviors can have very different monetary values.**

## Don't Confuse "Commercial Intent" With "Engagement"

A user with strong commercial intent isn't automatically your most valuable user.

This depends entirely on the funnel.

Imagine someone searching for information about a home service.

One user is ready to call a provider immediately.

Another user is researching the topic, reading several articles, comparing options, and clicking through related searches.

If your business model depends on content engagement and monetized search activity, the second user may be more valuable to your funnel—even though they have not demonstrated immediate purchase intent.

This is why **user intent needs to be evaluated in the context of the monetization model.**

There is no universally superior optimization event.

There is only the event that is most aligned with the behavior you are trying to generate.

## Creative and Optimization Work Together

Optimization cannot be separated completely from creative.

Your creative determines **who wants to click**.

Your optimization event helps determine **which types of users TikTok looks for**.

Your landing page determines **what happens after the click**.

And your monetization model determines **which behaviors actually create revenue.**

Think about the complete system:

**Creative → Audience → Optimization → Landing Page → User Behavior → Revenue**

If one part doesn't match the others, performance can suffer.

For example, you might have an excellent creative with a strong hook and a high CTR.

But if the optimization event attracts users who don't behave well once they reach the article, the campaign can still produce poor economics.

Likewise, an excellent landing page won't solve the problem if the campaign is consistently finding the wrong type of user.

## The Article Is Part of the Funnel

This becomes especially important in content arbitrage.

When the destination is an informational article, **the article itself becomes part of the conversion path.**

The job of the ad is not necessarily to sell the user something.

The job may simply be to create enough curiosity and relevance to get the right person onto the page.

Then the article needs to fulfill that expectation.

A strong funnel can therefore look like:

**Relevant Creative**

↓

**Interested User**

↓

**Article Consumption**

↓

**Related Search Interaction**

↓

**Additional Content / Monetized Interaction**

The ad and the article have to make sense together.

If the creative promises information but the landing page immediately feels like a sales page, the user experience breaks.

For RSOC-style funnels, this distinction is particularly important because the website's role is centered around **informational content and related search experiences rather than directly selling a product or service.**

## How We Think About Optimization at Impact Waves

At Impact Waves, we don't treat the optimization event as a default setting.

We start with the funnel.

**What are we buying?**

Traffic.

**What do we need that traffic to do?**

That depends on the monetization model.

**What behavior generates revenue?**

That's the behavior we want to teach the platform to find.

This means the same creative can potentially require a different optimization strategy depending on where the traffic is going and how that traffic is monetized.

The important question isn't:

**"Which optimization event gets the cheapest CPA?"**

It's:

**"Which optimization event produces the users that create the most valuable traffic?"**

That distinction can completely change how you evaluate a TikTok campaign.

## The Bottom Line

A campaign can have:

- A great creative
- Cheap CPM
- Strong CTR
- Cheap CPC

…and still lose money.

The missing piece may be **the quality of the users being delivered after the click.**

For traffic arbitrage, optimization should therefore be treated as part of the overall strategy.

**Don't optimize for the action you wish the user would eventually take.**

**Optimize for the behavior that actually creates value in your funnel.**

When the creative, optimization event, landing page, user intent, and monetization model all point in the same direction, TikTok becomes much easier to understand—and much more powerful to scale.
